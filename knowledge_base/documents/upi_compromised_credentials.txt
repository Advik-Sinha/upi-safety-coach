
TITLE: Responding to Exposed UPI Credentials
SOURCE ORGANISATION: National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) and Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
SOURCE TYPE: Curated summary of official guidance

SOURCE 1:
Organisation: NPCI
Title: MyUPI - User Controls and Safety Switch

Organisation: RBI

CONTENT:

If a person has disclosed their UPI PIN or suspects that someone may misuse their UPI access, they should take the following precautions:

1. Contact their bank immediately through an official banking app, verified bank website, official helpline or bank branch.
2. Explain that their UPI credentials may have been exposed and ask the bank to secure the account and UPI access.
3. If an unauthorised transaction has occurred, report it to the bank immediately. Ask for a complaint acknowledgement or reference number.
4. Check recent account and UPI transactions for activity that they did not authorise.
5. Do not disclose the UPI PIN, OTP, password or other banking credentials to anyone claiming to provide assistance.
6. Report financial cyber fraud promptly through India's official cybercrime helpline 1930 or https://cybercrime.gov.in/.

NPCI SAFETY SWITCH:

NPCI's MyUPI describes a Safety Switch that can request the decline of UPI debit transactions across selected linked accounts.

Important limitations:
- Availability depends on access through a supported UPI app.
- The Safety Switch is an additional safeguard.
- It does not replace contacting the bank or issuer.
- The bank or issuer should be contacted for transaction blocking, account-level controls and fraud reporting.

RBI GUIDANCE:

RBI advises customers to notify their bank immediately after an unauthorised electronic banking transaction. Delaying notification can increase the risk of loss.

The bank should provide an acknowledgement of the complaint. Customer liability depends on the circumstances of the transaction and applicable RBI directions.

IMPORTANT:

This document is a curated summary, not a verbatim reproduction of either organisation's publication. It does not establish that every UPI app supports the Safety Switch or that every exposed-PIN incident has the same outcome.
